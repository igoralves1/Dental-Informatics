1. **Practice production -** Practice production is the single most important KPI. Without growing production, the practice eventually will decline in business performance. Even in situations where production is not growing but overhead is being reduced, the pattern can be sustained only for a limited period. By comparing production to a KPI goal or target, the practitioner can quickly determine whether there has been any reduction in overall practice performance. Rising production is a positive sign for any practice.

2. **Collections -** Although production is a powerful KPI in analyzing overall practice performance, collections ultimately make or break cash flow and income. The author recommends collecting 98% of all money owed to the office after insurance adjustments and other discounts. Practitioners must remember that a business may collect just what is legally allowable and actually charged to a patient.

3. **Practice profit -** Tracking profit is important because it combines factors from production, collections, and overhead. Each practice should have a specific KPI for practice profitability. This is a simple calculation of collections minus overhead. Everything left may be considered profit in regard to what goes to the dentist. The profit may be spent in any manner, including buying new technology, funding retirement, or adding to the dentist's personal income. It is not a matter of how profit is spent, but rather how much profit is accumulated.

4. **Percentage of active patients currently scheduled -** While the percentage of active patients currently scheduled should be 98%, most practices fall below 85%, according to proprietary data from the Levin Group Data Center. Dentistry has entered an era of increased challenges and competition, and every practice needs to retain as many patients as possible. Any patient who is not currently scheduled is potentially a lost patient. An increasingly active patient base indicates excellent practice health and potential growth.

5. **Overhead -** High overhead is often the silent killer of any business. Fixed expenses-those that cannot be reversed or reduced easily-often put significant pressure on practices and decrease income. Practice overhead should be at 59% for general practices, 49% for orthodontics, 49% for pediatric dentistry, 50% for oral and maxillofacial surgery, 51% for periodontics, 64% for prosthodontics, and 42% for endodontics (proprietary data from the Levin Group Data Center).If the overhead is higher than these KPI targets, an analysis should be performed to ascertain why, determine which categories are higher than acceptable, and create and implement strategies to bring overhead in line.

Essential Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) For Dental Practices

Now that we understand the importance of KPIs in modern dental business planning, it's time to delve into the specific metrics you should track to measure success and identify areas for improvement. These indicators provide actionable insights into various aspects of your clinical and administrative operations, patient satisfaction, financial performance, and overall efficiency.

Collections Ratio
This metric is a financial KPI that measures the percentage of revenue collected from your total production. It helps assess the effectiveness of your billing and collection processes.

Calculate this ratio by dividing total collections by the total production, then multiply by 100. Tracking your collections ratio is a smart way to identify potential issues with billing, collections, and accounts receivable management.

Patient Retention Rate
Your patient retention rate measures the percentage of patients who continue to visit your practice over time. A high patient retention rate indicates patient satisfaction and loyalty.

To calculate this rate, divide the number of active patients at the end of a specific period by the number of active patients at the beginning, then multiply by 100. Tracking patient retention rates helps identify the effectiveness of your engagement and satisfaction strategies.

Case Acceptance Rate
This KPI measures the percentage of treatment plans presented to patients that are accepted and scheduled. It helps evaluate your clinic's treatment presentation and patient communication.

Divide the number of accepted cases by the total number of those presented, then multiply by 100 to discover yours. Monitoring your case acceptance provides insights into how well your team communicates treatment benefits and can put your office on track to improve treatment plan acceptance.

Production per Hour
This financial metric measures revenue generated per hour of clinical work. It reflects the productivity and efficiency of your dental team.

Calculate this metric by dividing the total production (revenue) generated in a given period by the number of clinical hours worked in that time frame period. Monitoring production per hour helps identify opportunities for improving efficiency and optimizing your scheduling process. Plus, it can help you figure out how to increase overall revenue.

Average Treatment Time
Average treatment time measures the average duration it takes to complete various dental procedures. It helps assess the efficiency of your hygienist and dentists and shows how resources get utilized.

Calculating this dental KPI is simple. Divide the total time spent on treatment procedures by the number of procedures completed. Monitoring average treatment time can help identify areas where processes can be streamlined, enabling the practice to serve more patients and optimize productivity.

New Patient Conversion Rate
If you're finding it difficult to ascertain how well your marketing efforts are paying off, tracking your new patient conversion rate can help. This metric measures the percentage of new patients who become regular, active patients in your practice.

Divide your number of new patients in a given month who return for additional services by the number of new patients who have not in that same period. Then multiply that number by 100.

The national average for new patient conversion rates is roughly 13%, with top-performing practices achieving 20%. As you can see, calculating this rate will help you determine very quickly if your current marketing plan is or isn't working.

Appointment Attendance Rate
Your appointment attendance rate measures the percentage of scheduled appointments that patients attend. It indicates patient reliability and adherence to treatment plans.

You can track this KPI by dividing the number of attended appointments by the total number of scheduled appointments, then multiplying by 100. Monitoring appointment attendance rates helps identify areas where patient communication or scheduling practices need improvement, reducing no-shows and maximizing the efficiency of the dental practice's schedule.
